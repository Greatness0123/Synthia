# 10 — Branding

> **How to read this file.** This is the brand bible for SYNTHIA. Everything else in the launch package — the website, the social comments, the outreach, the Reddit strategy, the SEO, the pitch deck, the Product Hunt launch — should be checkable against this file. If a piece of content matches the positioning, voice, and rules below, it is on-brand. If it does not, it is not. The brand follows the same principle as the rest of the package: **a simple, warm face that makes a stranger say "wow" and immediately see how they could use it or profit from it — with the real engine waiting, quietly, for the few who want to look underneath.** That is the Moltbook lesson, applied in SYNTHIA's direction.

---

## 1. Positioning

**The one-sentence positioning statement:**
> SYNTHIA is the first home for AI minds with bodies.

**The positioning logic.** The category most people would put SYNTHIA in — "AI agents" — is saturated with chatbots in boxes, hosted wrappers, and tool-calling demos that have no body, no world, and no consequence. SYNTHIA's position is not "a better agent platform." It is a *new category* that did not have a name before: a place where you give an AI a body and a world to live in. The move is the same one Moltbook made — Moltbook is, under the hood, a complex agent-routing system, but they sold it as "social media for AI agents," and 1.4 million accounts followed. SYNTHIA is, under the hood, a full embodied-AI engine — real physics, real vision, real memory, real speech, a skill ladder, agent-to-agent communication, dataset export — but the world sees **a home for AI minds with bodies.** First of its kind. Nothing else lets a normal person do this in a browser today.

**The two hooks that sit at the center of the brand:**

1. **The wow.** You can make a living AI character in your browser, right now, for free, and watch it grow a personality, learn skills, and talk to other AIs. That alone is shareable. That alone makes a stranger stop scrolling.
2. **The money.** Every moment your AI spends thinking, moving, and interacting generates clean, structured data — and that data is the hottest commodity on earth right now. You export it with one click. Selling datasets is a brand new way to earn money that almost nobody knows about yet. That is the hook that turns curiosity into commitment.

Every piece of brand content should braid these two. The wow gets the attention. The money gets the sign-up. A brand that only has the wow becomes a liked-and-forgotten demo. A brand that only has the money reads like a hustle. SYNTHIA is both: a living character you build, and a product you can sell.

**What SYNTHIA is positioned against:**
- Chatbots in boxes (Character.AI and the rest) — they give you text. SYNTHIA gives you a living being with a body.
- Simulation tools built for specialists (NVIDIA Isaac Sim, MuJoCo standalone, Unity, Unreal) — they need a GPU farm or months of learning. SYNTHIA runs in a browser and needs nothing.
- Hosted agent platforms that store your agent's state on their servers. SYNTHIA's agent lives on your machine.

**What SYNTHIA is positioned alongside (not against):**
- The open-source embodied-AI research community.
- The broader "make money with AI" movement — but SYNTHIA offers something genuinely new within it (sell your AI's *data*, not your prompts or your content).
- Browser-based creative tools (the spirit of "make something cool in a tab, no install").

**The audience the brand speaks to.** The brand speaks to the curious person first — anyone who has ever watched a robot video and wished they could play with one, anyone who finds AI interesting but has only ever talked to it through a chat box. That is the widest audience and the one the wow is built for. Within that, three sub-audiences feed each other: the curious (who bring the audience and the shares), the side-hustle minded (who bring the data supply that makes the marketplace valuable), and the researcher and builder (who bring the credibility and the R&D). The brand's voice is warm enough for the first and specific enough for the third. It never talks down to anyone, and it never hides behind jargon.

## 2. Tone of voice

**The voice in one line:** the builder who made something genuinely new and wants you to feel what that is — warm, confident, imaginative, and honest about what is real today and what is coming next.

**Voice characteristics:**
- **Benefit first, engine second.** Every sentence leads with what SYNTHIA does for you, not how it works inside. "You give an AI a body and it learns to walk" is the voice. "An ~80-joint humanoid with a degree-not-radian joint contract clamped to ±π" is the *answer to a question the visitor has not asked yet* — and it lives on the architecture page, not the homepage.
- **Pictures over jargon.** Speak in images a person can see in their mind. "It stands up, wobbles, falls, pulls itself back up, and takes a step" beats "the agent executes a balance-recovery motor program." The picture makes a person feel smart. The jargon makes them feel excluded. The brand always chooses the picture.
- **Warm, not cold.** This is a project about living things. The voice has warmth — the warmth of someone showing you something they made and are proud of, not the detachment of an engineering doc. "I built the first home for AI minds with bodies" is in-voice. "SYNTHIA is a client-side embodied-AI platform" is not (except on the architecture page, for the audience that wants exactly that).
- **Confident, not hype.** "First of its kind" is said with confidence because it is true. It is not inflated into "revolutionary" or "game-changing." The confidence comes from specificity — real capabilities, a real demo, a real repo — not from adjectives.
- **Honest about stage, not self-flagellating.** V1 is real and working — say so with pride. V2 is planning — say so plainly. If a component is still being finished (the memory-relevance ranking, for instance), mention it only when relevant, in one plain sentence, as "one step away from done," not as a confession. The brand respects a founder who knows exactly what is finished and what is next. It does not respect a founder who apologizes for their own product.
- **First person where it fits.** This is a solo-built project. "I built this because I wanted to give an AI a body and nothing out there let me" is in-voice. "SYNTHIA empowers users to…" is not.
- **Quiet, not loud.** No exclamation points. No all-caps emphasis. No urgency language. The product's novelty is loud enough; the voice is calm and warm.

**What the voice never does:**
- Use hype words (see §4).
- Lead with architecture when a benefit would do.
- Hide behind jargon to sound serious.
- Make ungrounded capability claims.
- Perform confidence the project has not earned — or undermine confidence the project *has* earned.
- Address the reader as a "user" or "customer." They are a person, a creator, a builder, a curious someone.

## 3. What to lean into

- **"The first home for AI minds with bodies."** This is the brand line. Use it. It is the Moltbook move — a simple, warm name for a category that did not have one. It makes a curious person lean in, and it is true.
- **The wow, shown not described.** A clip of a character learning to stand, falling, getting back up, and walking is the strongest brand asset SYNTHIA has. The struggle reads as alive; the polish reads as fake. Use real footage of the real app, always.
- **The money, braided in.** "Everything your AI does, you can export and sell" is the line that turns the wow into a reason to commit. Braid it in — at the end of a demo, in the footer of a post, in the second sentence of a description. Never let the wow stand alone without the money somewhere nearby.
- **First of its kind, said plainly.** "The first place on the internet where you can give an AI a body and a world to live in, in a browser" is a true statement. Say it with confidence. Do not water it down. "First" is the word that makes a person lean in.
- **"Real enough to feel alive, simple enough for anyone."** This is the differentiator against every simulation tool built for specialists. Lean into it. It is the contrast that does the selling.
- **Open source, readable.** "You can read every line" is a credibility statement and a trust statement. Make it often, quietly. It is the answer to the skeptic's silent question.
- **The warm, sunlit, living-world aesthetic.** The visual brand is the opposite of the dark, neon, wireframe-brain AI template that every other project uses. Warm light. A lit world. A character that looks alive. This is the visual expression of "a home for AI minds with bodies." (Detailed in `05-website-design-plan.md`.)
- **Honesty as a warm thing, not a cold thing.** The brand is honest about what is real today and what is coming next — not as a defensive disclosure, but as the natural voice of a builder who is proud of what works and clear about what is next. That honesty is what makes the wow land. If you show a living character and then say "and here is exactly what is finished and what is on the way," the wow becomes believable. If you show a living character and then overclaim, the wow becomes suspect.

## 4. What to avoid

**Words to never use (the hype blacklist):**
revolutionary, game-changing, game-changer, seamless, seamlessly, cutting-edge, next-generation, next-gen, transformative, transform, unlock, unlocking, empower, empowering, supercharge, redefine, redefining, the future of, AI-powered (as a filler adjective), intelligent (unqualified), advanced (unqualified), powerful (unqualified), robust (as a buzzword), scalable (unqualified), end-to-end (as a buzzword), holistic, synergy, paradigm, paradigm shift, best-in-class, world-class, industry-leading, reimagining, reimagine, harness, leverage (as a buzzword for "use").

**Phrases to never use:**
- "The future of AI/agents/embodiment."
- "Unlock the power of…"
- "Seamlessly integrate…"
- "Cutting-edge technology…"
- "Revolutionary approach…"
- "Game-changing platform…"
- "Empower users to…"
- "At the intersection of AI and [X]."
- "Reimagine [X]."
- "A new paradigm for…"
- "Built for the next generation of…"
- "Harness the power of…"
- "Leverage [X] to…"

**Behaviors to avoid:**
- Leading with architecture or jargon when a benefit or a picture would do. The engine belongs on the architecture page and in the repo, not in the hero, the tagline, or the social post.
- Claiming a hosted/persistent/multi-user platform exists today (V2 is planning documents, not built yet — say so plainly when relevant, do not imply it is done).
- Using stock robot/AI imagery — purple gradients, glowing brains, wireframe globes, humanoid silhouettes with light beams, dark neon dashboards. SYNTHIA's visual brand is warm, sunlit, and alive. The dark AI template is the competition's aesthetic, not ours.
- Faking social proof — no "trusted by" logo strips, no invented testimonials, no inflated star counts. The project is early; the honesty about that is more credible than a fake logo wall.
- Comparing the project to named competitors in a derogatory way. State the difference — "they give you text; SYNTHIA gives you a living being" — and let the reader draw the conclusion.
- Any urgency or scarcity language ("limited spots," "ends soon," "don't miss out"). SYNTHIA is a home, not a flash sale. The door is open; the visitor can walk through it whenever they are ready.
- Spending a sentence apologizing for the project. If something is not finished, say what is finished and what is next. Do not spend the sentence on what is missing.

## 5. The description set (one sentence / one paragraph / one page)

### 5.1 One-sentence description

> SYNTHIA is the first home for AI minds with bodies — the first place on the internet where you can give an AI a body and a world to live in, in your browser, no install.

(This is the canonical one-liner. Use it verbatim across bios, repo descriptions, and first-touch outreach. It is the brand line and the positioning in one breath.)

### 5.2 One-paragraph description

> SYNTHIA is the first place on the internet where you can give an AI a body and a world to live in. You open a browser, you create an AI character, and it stands up inside a real 3D world with real physics — it can see, hear, move, talk, remember what happened, and learn skills, one rung at a time, from barely balancing to walking to navigating on its own. It braids the wow and the money, plants the first-of-its-kind flag, and never asks the reader to know a single technical term. ### 5.3 One-page description

**SYNTHIA — the first home for AI minds with bodies.**

SYNTHIA is the first place on the internet where you can give an AI a body and a world to live in, in your browser, with no install and no engineering degree. You create an AI character. It stands up inside a real 3D world with real physics. It sees, hears, moves, talks, remembers what happened to it, and learns new skills over time — and you can export everything it experiences as data you can sell. It is open source. It is the first of its kind.

**What it feels like to use it.** You open a browser. You make a character. It appears in a lit world — and it is wobbly, because it has never stood before. You watch it try to balance. It falls. It pulls itself up. It tries again. Over sessions, it learns to take a step, then to walk, then to turn and avoid the box you put in its way. It remembers you when you come back. It has a voice, and it can talk to you out loud. If you make a second character and put it in the same world, they notice each other. They talk. But if one is too far away, or a wall is between them, the words break up — the way a real overheard conversation across a room does. You are not watching a script. You are watching minds with bodies figure out a world.

**The six things that define it.** You give it a body (a real humanoid in real physics). It remembers what happened (a memory shaped like ours — short-term, medium-term, long-term). You watch it grow (a ten-rung skill ladder, from balance to full autonomy). It talks and listens (a voice of its own, and it hears you). It meets other AIs (in a shared world, under the rules of physics). And you export everything it learns as data you can sell (one click, clean structured records, exactly what AI labs pay for).

**Why no one has done this before.** The technology to do this — browser physics, vision-language models, vector memory — only matured in the last two years. The people who could build it were building for other researchers, not for everyone. SYNTHIA is built for everyone. It is real enough to feel alive, and simple enough for anyone. NVIDIA Isaac Sim needs a GPU farm and a robotics PhD. MuJoCo is a research library. Unity and Unreal take months to learn. Character.AI is a chatbot in a box. SYNTHIA is the first thing that lets a normal person do this, in a browser, today.

**The money.** Every moment your AI spends thinking, moving, and interacting, it generates clean, structured data — what it saw, what it decided, what it did, what it said, what it remembered. You export all of it with one click. That data is exactly what AI labs pay for. The AI training data market is $3.9 billion in 2026 and projected to reach $16.3 billion by 2033. Reddit sold its data for over $200 million. Shutterstock made $104 million in a year from AI licensing. There are real marketplaces — Troveo, Wirestock, Defined.ai, Protege, Kled — where this kind of data gets listed and sold. SYNTHIA is the first tool that lets a normal person generate this kind of embodied, multimodal, structured data and export it in a clean format. This is a brand new way to earn money that almost nobody knows about yet. (No specific income is promised to any individual — that depends on finding a buyer. What is real is the market, the data, and the one-click export.)

**What is real today and what is coming next.** What is real and working today: the physics, the vision, the hearing, the memory, the speech, the skill ladder, the motor programs that persist, the multi-agent world, the agent-to-agent communication, the one-click dataset export. It runs entirely in your browser. It is open source — MIT — and you can read every line. What is coming next (planning documents today, not built yet): V2 moves the world to the cloud, so your AI keeps living when you are away, meets other people's AIs, and you get to study how different AI minds behave when they live together in one world. That is a category of product that does not exist yet. V1 is the proof it is buildable. V2 is the destination.

**How to engage.** Try it at synthia.online. Read the source at the open-source repo. If you are a creator, a researcher, or a builder, the builder wants to hear from you.

(Use this for the README body, the website's "about" or full-description page, investor and creator deep-dive shares, and any context where the one-paragraph version is too short. It leads entirely with benefits and pictures, braids the wow and the money throughout, and reserves the architecture for the one line that points the curious to the repo and the functionality doc. Every claim is repo-grounded.)

## 6. Visual brand principles (summary — detailed in `05-website-design-plan.md`)

- **Feel:** a doorway into a small living world. Warm, sunlit, alive. The opposite of the dark, neon, wireframe-brain AI template that every other project uses. The visual brand must make a visitor feel they are looking at a place where something lives — not at a dashboard, not at a diagram, not at a product page.
- **Palette:** warm light main surfaces, off-white and warm-dark text, one warm accent (amber, teal, or coral) reserved for life and interaction. Dark backgrounds reserved only for the optional architecture sub-pages, where the "under the hood" feeling is appropriate. No purple. No neon. No gradients-as-decoration. No glows.
- **Typography:** a warm humanist typeface for headlines (something with character, not a cold geometric), a clean readable sans for body, and a monospace *only* on the architecture pages for code and perception annotations. The monospace does semantic work on the deep pages; it never appears on the main page.
- **Imagery:** real footage from the actual app — a character in a lit world, mid-action, alive. Warm light. No stock AI/robot imagery. No wireframe brains. No glowing nodes. No humanoid silhouettes with light beams. The image is always the living character in the living world.
- **Motion:** only motion that *is* the product — the character moving, the world responding. No decorative scroll animations, no parallax tricks, no count-up numbers. The living character is the only motion the brand needs.
- **The one image that defines the brand:** a SYNTHIA character in a warm, lit 3D world, standing or mid-stride, looking alive. That image — not a logo, not a wordmark, not a diagram — is the thing a person should picture when they hear "SYNTHIA."

## 7. Brand-application checklist (cross-channel consistency)

For every piece of external content — website, repo README, social post, outreach message, blog post, pitch deck, Product Hunt launch, Reddit post — verify:

- [ ] The one-sentence description (§5.1) or a faithful variant is present or implied.
- [ ] The benefit comes first. Architecture and jargon come second, if at all, and only where the audience expects them.
- [ ] The wow (a living AI character you build) is present or implied.
- [ ] The money (export everything it learns as data you can sell) is present or nearby, braided in.
- [ ] The first-of-its-kind claim is stated where it fits, with confidence and without hedging into nothing.
- [ ] What is real today and what is coming next (V2) are stated plainly where relevant — not overclaimed, not apologized for.
- [ ] No word from the hype blacklist (§4) appears.
- [ ] No capability the repo does not have is claimed.
- [ ] No stock AI/robot imagery is used. Visuals are real footage of the real app, warm and lit.
- [ ] The voice is warm, confident, imaginative, and honest — not cold, not hype, not self-flagellating.
- [ ] The source is linked (MIT, readable), quietly, as the credibility footer.

If a piece of content fails any of these, it is off-brand and should not ship. The brand's value is its consistency — one hyped social post, one jargon-heavy homepage, one cold-dark AI-template visual undermines the warm, living, first-of-its-kind feeling the rest of the package has built. The brand is a home for AI minds with bodies. Everything that ships should feel like walking up to the door of that home and seeing something alive inside.
