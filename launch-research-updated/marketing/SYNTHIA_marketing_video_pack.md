# SYNTHIA Marketing Video Pack

This pack is built from the actual positioning in the launch research: SYNTHIA is the first place on the internet where you can give an AI a body and a world to live in; the wow is the living AI; the money is the exportable training data; the aesthetic is warm, alive, and human rather than cold, neon-tech. Every script below is built for public-facing explanation, not a developer-only demo. Each is designed to land in 60–90 seconds and feel clear, premium, and emotionally engaging.

Use these as the editorial backbone for YouTube Shorts, Reels, TikToks, X/LinkedIn cutdowns, and website hero videos. They can be repurposed into 15-second hooks and 3-minute deep dives.

## Video 1 — The Cup Test: Embodiment vs Disembodied AI

Duration: 75–90 sec

Hook: A cup sits on a table. The camera slowly tracks from the cup to a person staring at it, then back. The voiceover: “Most AI can talk. Very few can actually do things.”

Script:

Voiceover:

“Most AI sits in a chat box. It writes words, answers questions, maybe sounds helpful. But this is different. This is embodiment.

Watch this. I can see the cup, I know what it is, I know I could pick it up. My brain tells my body to move. There is a choice, a thought, a delay, a decision. Now look at this other version of me. He is not staring at the cup. He is already in his world. He reaches for his bottle, takes a sip, keeps working.

The difference is simple: one AI is disembodied. It only talks. The other has a body, a world, a sense of space, and the ability to act.

SYNTHIA is the first place on the internet where you can give an AI that kind of body and world — in a browser, with no install, no GPU, no coding. 

This is not a chatbot upgrade. This is the next step after text. This is embodied AI.

Try it at runsynthia.online.”

Props:
- Real cup / bottle / desk lamp / office desk
- White mug, black bottle, monitor with simple workspace
- Optional second person using same wardrobe for clone effect

Props substitute:
- Use a coffee mug, phone charger, water bottle, and a desk setup in any room
- If you cannot get a second actor, shoot a split-screen with same person repeating the action in two takes

Camera angles:
- A: Overhead close-up of cup, shallow depth, slow push-in
- B: Medium close-up of performer staring at cup, face in profile, static shot with subtle silence
- C: 3/4 angle as performer turns to side and reveals a second “version” of themself
- D: Match cut back to first performer with a quick zoom to their face
- E: Tight shot of bottle reaching and drinking
- F: Final reveal on “Try it at runsynthia.online” with a clean product shot or browser UI

CapCut workflow:
1. Shoot all shots in 4K 24fps, lock exposure, keep natural light warm and soft.
2. Start with 1.5 sec of cup-only close-up; add a subtle lens blur or rack focus on the cup.
3. Cut to face with a 0.4 sec whip-pan or speed ramp.
4. Insert second performer with a split-screen or masked copy effect. Use a jump cut and slight color shift between takes to imply “another version.”
5. Use match-cut audio: the cup sound can become a subtle inhale before the second shot.
6. Add text overlays: “CHATBOT” on the first side, “EMBODIED AI” on the second side.
7. Transition: match dissolve + sound swish at each branch.
8. Audio: low, suspenseful music with a soft whoosh and a quiet room tone. Use a slight de-ess on VO.
9. Color: warm neutral; desaturate the “old AI” side slightly; add more natural skin tones and warm highlights in the “embodied” side.
10. Final text style: bold sans-serif, uppercase, 72–96 size; color: graphite black + warm amber accent.

## Recommended Global CapCut Settings

Use these as the default across the pack:

- Aspect ratio: 9:16 for Shorts/Reels/TikTok; 16:9 for YouTube / website hero
- Frame rate: 24 fps or 30 fps; 24 for cinematic, 30 for social faster pacing
- Shutter: 1/48 or 1/50 for natural motion blur
- White balance: warm daylight look; keep skin tones natural
- Exposure: slightly underexpose 0.2–0.4 to keep highlight detail and add drama
- Noise: minimal, 1–2% grain if needed
- Autofocus: use face tracking for talking head clips; manual for product hero shots
- Transition presets: match cut, slide, wipe, dissolve, whip-pan, frame crop
- Text style: clean sans-serif such as Montserrat, Poppins, Manrope, or Inter
- Font weights: 500–700; uppercase only for short labels; title case for voiceover-driven story copy
- Text color: charcoal black, warm off-white, soft amber, muted teal, with subtle drop shadow
- Motion graphics: low opacity; do not overpower the product footage
- Sound design: room tone + subtle synth + UI clicks + whoosh transitions; keep VO at -10 to -12 LUFS
