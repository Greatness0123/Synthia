# 10 — Branding

> **How to read this file.** This is the brand reference for SYNTHIA. Everything else in the launch package — the website, the social comments, the outreach, the Reddit strategy, the SEO, the pitch deck, the Product Hunt launch — should be checkable against this file. If a piece of content matches the positioning, voice, and rules below, it is on-brand. If it does not, it is not. The brand follows the same principle as the rest of the package: **a simple face that makes a stranger understand what SYNTHIA is and what they can do with it — with the real engine waiting, quietly, for the few who want to look underneath.** That is the Moltbook lesson, applied in SYNTHIA's direction.

---

## 1. Positioning

**The one-sentence positioning statement:**
> SYNTHIA is a world where an AI mind with a body learns to live.

**The positioning logic.** The category most people would put SYNTHIA in — "AI agents" — is saturated with chatbots in boxes, hosted wrappers, and tool-calling demos that have no body, no world, and no consequence. SYNTHIA's position is not "a better agent platform." It is a *new category* that did not have a name before: a place where an AI mind with a body lives in a world it can see, hear, and move through, and a human shapes that world and steers that mind. The move is the same one Moltbook made — Moltbook is, under the hood, a complex agent-routing system, but they sold it as "social media for AI agents," and 1.4 million accounts followed. SYNTHIA is, under the hood, a full embodied-AI engine — real physics, real vision, real memory, real speech, a skill ladder, agent-to-agent communication, dataset export — but the world sees **a world where an AI mind with a body learns to live.** First of its kind. Nothing else lets a normal person do this in a browser today.

**The two ideas that sit at the center of the brand:**

1. **What it is.** An AI mind with a body, living in a 3D world with real physics, right now, for free, in a browser. It sees, hears, moves, talks, remembers, and learns skills one rung at a time. That alone is shareable. That alone makes a stranger stop scrolling.
2. **What you can earn.** Every moment the AI spends thinking, moving, and interacting generates clean, structured data — and that data is the hottest commodity on earth right now. You export it with one click. Selling datasets is a brand new way to earn money that almost nobody knows about yet. That is the idea that turns curiosity into commitment.

Every piece of brand content should braid these two. What it is gets the attention. What you can earn gets the sign-up. A brand that only has what-it-is becomes a liked-and-forgotten demo. A brand that only has what-you-can-earn reads like a hustle. SYNTHIA is both: a world where an AI mind learns to live, and a product you can sell.

**What SYNTHIA is positioned against:**
- Chatbots in boxes (Character.AI and the rest) — they give you text. SYNTHIA gives you an AI with a body in a world.
- Simulation tools built for specialists (NVIDIA Isaac Sim, MuJoCo standalone, Unity, Unreal) — they need a GPU farm or months of learning. SYNTHIA runs in a browser and needs nothing.
- Hosted agent platforms that store your agent's state on their servers. SYNTHIA's AI lives on your machine.

**What SYNTHIA is positioned alongside (not against):**
- The open-source embodied-AI research community.
- The broader "make money with AI" movement — but SYNTHIA offers something genuinely new within it (sell your AI's *data*, not your prompts or your content).
- Browser-based creative tools (the spirit of "make something cool in a tab, no install").

**The audience the brand speaks to.** The brand speaks to the curious person first — anyone who has ever watched a robot video and wished they could try one, anyone who finds AI interesting but has only ever talked to it through a chat box. That is the widest audience. Within that, three sub-audiences feed each other: the curious (who bring the audience and the shares), the side-hustle minded (who bring the data supply that makes the marketplace valuable), and the researcher and builder (who bring the credibility and the R&D). The brand's voice is plain enough for the first and specific enough for the third. It never talks down to anyone, and it never hides behind jargon.

**One honesty rule that overrides everything else.** The AI does not know you exist. It does not see you, it does not wave at you, it does not have a relationship with you. It is a model in a world, learning to live. What you have is a steering surface — you build the world around it, you give it goals, you inject thoughts into its mind. That is the real value, and the brand never oversells it into false intimacy. Character.AI knows you exist because you are typing at it. SYNTHIA's AI does not, because you are not. The honesty about that distinction is what makes SYNTHIA credible.

## 2. Tone of voice

**The voice in one line:** the builder who made something genuinely new and wants you to understand what that is — plain, clear, and honest about what is real today and what is coming next.

**Voice characteristics:**
- **Benefit first, engine second.** Every sentence leads with what SYNTHIA does for you, not how it works inside. "You place an AI in a body and it learns to walk" is the voice. "An ~80-joint humanoid with a degree-not-radian joint contract clamped to ±π" is the *answer to a question the visitor has not asked yet* — and it lives on the architecture page, not the homepage.
- **Pictures over jargon.** Speak in images a person can see in their mind. "It stands up, wobbles, falls, pulls itself back up, and takes a step" beats "the agent executes a balance-recovery motor program." The picture makes a person feel smart. The jargon makes them feel excluded. The brand always chooses the picture.
- **Plain, not warm.** This is a project about an AI learning to live in a world. The voice is direct and factual, not warm or cozy. "I built a world where an AI mind with a body learns to live" is in-voice. "SYNTHIA is a client-side embodied-AI platform" is not (except on the architecture page, for the audience that wants exactly that). The voice is not cold either — it is the plainness of someone showing you something real and letting you judge it.
- **Confident, not hype.** "First of its kind" is said with confidence because it is true. It is not inflated into "revolutionary" or "game-changing." The confidence comes from specificity — real capabilities, a real demo, a real repo — not from adjectives.
- **Honest about stage, not self-flagellating.** V1 is real and working — say so plainly. V2 is planning — say so plainly. If a component is still being finished (the memory-relevance ranking, for instance), mention it only when relevant, in one plain sentence, as "one step away from done," not as a confession. The brand respects a founder who knows exactly what is finished and what is next. It does not respect a founder who apologizes for their own product.
- **First person where it fits.** This is a solo-built project. "I built this because I wanted to put an AI in a body and nothing out there let me" is in-voice. "SYNTHIA empowers users to…" is not.
- **Quiet, not loud.** No exclamation points. No all-caps emphasis. No urgency language. The product's novelty is loud enough; the voice is calm and plain.
- **Honest about what the AI is and is not.** The AI is a model in a world, learning to live. It is not your friend, it does not know you exist, it does not wave at you or miss you. Say what it is (a mind with a body, sensing and acting in a world you shape) and do not say what it is not (a companion that knows you). This honesty is a feature, not a limitation — it is what separates SYNTHIA from the chatbots that pretend.

**What the voice never does:**
- Use hype words (see §4).
- Lead with architecture when a benefit would do.
- Hide behind jargon to sound serious.
- Make ungrounded capability claims.
- Perform confidence the project has not earned — or undermine confidence the project *has* earned.
- Claim the AI knows you, sees you, waves at you, remembers you as a person, or has a relationship with you.
- Address the reader as a "user" or "customer." They are a person, a creator, a builder, a curious someone.
- Use game language — "game," "gameplay," "level," "score," "play" (as in "playing with the AI"), "playground," "sandbox" (as a game frame). SYNTHIA is not a game. It is a world where an AI mind learns to live.

## 3. What to lean into

- **"A world where an AI mind with a body learns to live."** This is the brand line. Use it. It is the Moltbook move — a simple name for a category that did not have one. It makes a curious person lean in, and it is true. It captures what the AI is (a mind with a body), what it does (learns to live), and where (a world). It does not oversell — it does not claim the AI knows you or has a relationship with you.
- **What it is, shown not described.** A clip of an AI learning to stand, falling, getting back up, and walking is the strongest brand asset SYNTHIA has. The struggle reads as real; the polish reads as fake. Use real footage of the real app, always.
- **What you can earn, braided in.** "Everything the AI does, you can export and sell" is the line that turns curiosity into a reason to commit. Braid it in — at the end of a demo, in the footer of a post, in the second sentence of a description. Never let what-it-is stand alone without what-you-can-earn somewhere nearby.
- **First of its kind, said plainly.** "The first place on the internet where you can place an AI in a body and a world, in a browser" is a true statement. Say it with confidence. Do not water it down. "First" is the word that makes a person lean in.
- **"Real enough to behave like something alive, simple enough for anyone."** This is the differentiator against every simulation tool built for specialists. Lean into it. It is the contrast that does the selling.
- **The human's role, made plain.** You build the world around the AI. You give it goals. You inject thoughts into its mind. You measure whether it succeeded. You export what it generates. The brand should make the human's agency as visible as the AI's behavior — because the steering surface is the real product, not a pet.
- **Open source, readable.** "You can read every line" is a credibility statement and a trust statement. Make it often, quietly. It is the answer to the skeptic's silent question.
- **The lit, plain, real aesthetic.** The visual brand is the opposite of the dark, neon, wireframe-brain AI template that every other project uses. Lit. Plain. Real. A world you can see. An AI that looks like it is in that world. This is the visual expression of "a world where an AI mind with a body learns to live." (Detailed in `05-website-design-plan.md`.)
- **Honesty as the thing that makes it credible.** The brand is honest about what is real today and what is coming next — not as a defensive disclosure, but as the natural voice of a builder who is clear about what works and what is next. That honesty is what makes the whole thing land. If you show an AI learning to live and then say "and here is exactly what is finished and what is on the way," it becomes believable. If you show it and then overclaim, it becomes suspect.

## 4. What to avoid

**Words to never use (the hype blacklist):**
revolutionary, game-changing, game-changer, seamless, seamlessly, cutting-edge, next-generation, next-gen, transformative, transform, unlock, unlocking, empower, empowering, supercharge, redefine, redefining, the future of, AI-powered (as a filler adjective), intelligent (unqualified), advanced (unqualified), powerful (unqualified), robust (as a buzzword), scalable (unqualified), end-to-end (as a buzzword), holistic, synergy, paradigm, paradigm shift, best-in-class, world-class, industry-leading, reimagining, reimagine, harness, leverage (as a buzzword for "use").

**Game words to never use:**
game, gameplay, level, score, play (as in "playing with the AI"), playground, sandbox (as a game frame), quest, win, lose, respawn, spawn a character (the app uses "spawn" mechanically for the agent — that is fine in technical contexts; in marketing, say "place an AI in a world" instead).

**Phrases to never use:**
- "The future of AI/agents/embodiment."
- "Unlock the power of…"
- "Seamlessly integrate…"
- "Cutting-edge technology…"
- "Revolutionary approach…"
- "Game-changing platform…"
- "Empower users to…"
- "At the intersection of AI and [X]."
- "Reimagine [X]."
- "A new paradigm for…"
- "Built for the next generation of…"
- "Harness the power of…"
- "Leverage [X] to…"
- "Build a character" or "build an AI character" (the AI is placed into a world, not built from scratch by the user).

**Behaviors to avoid:**
- Leading with architecture or jargon when a benefit or a picture would do. The engine belongs on the architecture page and in the repo, not in the hero, the tagline, or the social post.
- Claiming the AI knows you, sees you, waves at you, remembers you as a person, or has a relationship with you. It does not. It is a model in a world.
- Claiming a hosted/persistent/multi-user platform exists today (V2 is planning documents, not built yet — say so plainly when relevant, do not imply it is done).
- Using stock robot/AI imagery — purple gradients, glowing brains, wireframe globes, humanoid silhouettes with light beams, dark neon dashboards. SYNTHIA's visual brand is lit, plain, and real. The dark AI template is the competition's aesthetic, not ours.
- Faking social proof — no "trusted by" logo strips, no invented testimonials, no inflated star counts. The project is early; the honesty about that is more credible than a fake logo wall.
- Comparing the project to named competitors in a derogatory way. State the difference — "they give you text; SYNTHIA gives you an AI with a body in a world" — and let the reader draw the conclusion.
- Any urgency or scarcity language ("limited spots," "ends soon," "don't miss out"). SYNTHIA is a world, not a flash sale. The door is open; the visitor can walk through it whenever they are ready.
- Spending a sentence apologizing for the project. If something is not finished, say what is finished and what is next. Do not spend the sentence on what is missing.
- Framing SYNTHIA as a game or using game language. It is not a game. It is a world where an AI mind with a body learns to live. Game framing attracts the wrong audience and misrepresents what the software is.

**SYNTHIA — a world where an AI mind with a body learns to live.**

When you come back, it picks up where it left off. It has a voice, and you can talk to it out loud. If you place a second AI in the same world, they notice each other. They talk. What you do is shape the world around it, give it goals, and inject thoughts into its mind.

**Why no one has done this before.** The technology to do this — browser physics, vision-language models, vector memory — only matured in the last two years. The people who could build it were building for other researchers, not for everyone. SYNTHIA is built for everyone. It is real enough to behave like something alive, and simple enough for anyone. NVIDIA Isaac Sim needs a GPU farm and a robotics PhD. MuJoCo is a research library. Unity and Unreal take months to learn. Character.AI is a chatbot in a box. SYNTHIA is the first thing that lets a normal person do this, in a browser, today.

**What you can earn.** Every moment the AI spends thinking, moving, and interacting, it generates clean, structured data — what it saw, what it decided, what it did, what it said, what it remembered. You export all of it with one click. That data is exactly what AI labs pay for. The AI training data market is $3.9 billion in 2026 and projected to reach $16.3 billion by 2033. Reddit sold its data for over $200 million. Shutterstock made $104 million in a year from AI licensing. There are real marketplaces — Troveo, Wirestock, Defined.ai, Protege, Kled — where this kind of data gets listed and sold. SYNTHIA is the first tool that lets a normal person generate this kind of embodied, multimodal, structured data and export it in a clean format. This is a brand new way to earn money that almost nobody knows about yet. (No specific income is promised to any individual — that depends on finding a buyer. What is real is the market, the data, and the one-click export.)

**What is real today and what is coming next.** What is real and working today: the physics, the vision, the hearing, the memory, the speech, the skill ladder, the motor programs that persist, the multi-agent world, the agent-to-agent communication, the one-click dataset export. What is coming next (planning documents today, not built yet): V2 moves the world to the cloud, so the AI keeps living when you are away, meets other people's AIs, and you get to study how different AI minds behave when they live together in one world. That is a category of product that does not exist yet. V1 is the proof it is buildable. V2 is the destination.

**How to engage.** Try it at synthia.online. Read the source at the open-source repo. If you are a creator, a researcher, or a builder, the builder wants to hear from you.

## 6. Visual brand principles (summary — detailed in `05-website-design-plan.md`)

- **Feel:** a doorway into a small world where something is learning to live. Lit, plain, real. The opposite of the dark, neon, wireframe-brain AI template that every other project uses. The visual brand must make a visitor feel they are looking at a place where something real is happening — not at a dashboard, not at a diagram, not at a product page.
- **Palette:** lit main surfaces, off-white and dark text, one accent (amber, teal, or coral) reserved for life and interaction. Dark backgrounds reserved only for the optional architecture sub-pages, where the "under the hood" feeling is appropriate. No purple. No neon. No gradients-as-decoration. No glows.
- **Typography:** a plain humanist typeface for headlines (something with character, not a cold geometric), a clean readable sans for body, and a monospace *only* on the architecture pages for code and perception annotations. The monospace does semantic work on the deep pages; it never appears on the main page.
- **Imagery:** real footage from the actual app — an AI in a lit world, mid-action. No stock AI/robot imagery. No wireframe brains. No glowing nodes. No humanoid silhouettes with light beams. The image is always the AI in the world.
- **Motion:** only motion that *is* the product — the AI moving, the world responding. No decorative scroll animations, no parallax tricks, no count-up numbers. The AI in the world is the only motion the brand needs.
- **The one image that defines the brand:** a SYNTHIA AI in a lit 3D world, standing or mid-stride, looking like it is in that world. That image — not a logo, not a wordmark, not a diagram — is the thing a person should picture when they hear "SYNTHIA."

## 7. Brand-application checklist (cross-channel consistency)

For every piece of external content — website, repo README, social post, outreach message, blog post, pitch deck, Product Hunt launch, Reddit post — verify:

- [ ] The one-sentence description (§5.1) or a faithful variant is present or implied.
- [ ] The benefit comes first. Architecture and jargon come second, if at all, and only where the audience expects them.
- [ ] What it is (an AI mind with a body in a world, learning to live) is present or implied.
- [ ] What you can earn (export everything it does as data you can sell) is present or nearby, braided in.
- [ ] The human's role (build the world, set goals, inject thoughts, measure success, export) is visible, not buried.
- [ ] The first-of-its-kind claim is stated where it fits, with confidence and without hedging into nothing.
- [ ] What is real today and what is coming next (V2) are stated plainly where relevant — not overclaimed, not apologized for.
- [ ] No word from the hype blacklist (§4) appears.
- [ ] No game word from the game-word list (§4) appears.
- [ ] No claim that the AI knows you, sees you, waves at you, remembers you as a person, or has a relationship with you.
- [ ] No capability the repo does not have is claimed.
- [ ] No stock AI/robot imagery is used. Visuals are real footage of the real app, lit and plain.
- [ ] The voice is plain, clear, and honest — not warm, not cold, not hype, not self-flagellating.
- [ ] The source is linked (MIT, readable), quietly, as the credibility footer.

If a piece of content fails any of these, it is off-brand and should not ship. The brand's value is its consistency — one hyped social post, one game-framed homepage, one overclaimed "the AI knows you" line, one jargon-heavy page, one dark AI-template visual undermines the plain, real, first-of-its-kind feeling the rest of the package has built. The brand is a world where an AI mind with a body learns to live. Everything that ships should feel like looking into that world and seeing something real inside.
